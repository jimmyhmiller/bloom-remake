# S25 — tiered latency

The user (2026-10-07, question tool): after S24's merge, "cut latency": the tiered engine's single-record latency
(Kafka end-to-end p50 9 ms against the in-memory engine's 4 ms, after 300000 records produced). Design:
docs/design/DATABASE.md §7.

## Finding it

`perf` on the brokers during `kafka-e2e-latency` after a 300000-record produce: ~20% of the engine in range probes
read from the tree. A temporary count of the tree's range reads by table and probe shape found one: `batch` probed
on `(tid, part)` with `base <= off`, ~3500 rows a probe, millions over the run. `fetch_first` means to find the batch
through `batch_chunk` (its chunk index, a few rows); the planner chose the `batch` range instead, because a tiered
table's estimates were guesses (an eighth of the table for any probe not on the key, a sixteenth of that for a
range), where an in-memory store reports its index's real bucket sizes.

## What changed

- **Learned estimates.** A tiered table learns, by the probe's columns, the rows a prefix holds (an index's average
  bucket, as an in-memory store knows it; a prefix too large to keep counts as large) and the rows a range of a
  prefix gave (`Tiered::observe`); the planner asks `Store::estimate_probe(cols, range)`. A prefix is costed at its
  learned rows (all rows for no columns, one for the key, one for columns not seen yet: tried, then known); a range
  at the larger of a sixteenth of its prefix (the in-memory reckoning) and what ranges of its shape gave (a range
  read from the cold side costs what it gives).

  The first cut learned only what each probe shape gave. The full tier caught it: `raft_kv::join_work_per_tick_is_
  flat_as_the_log_grows` (78 rows per tick over 8 s against 47.6 over 2 s): `write_before`'s probe of `log` by key
  with a range below the entry had small learned results while its prefix grew with the log, and was chosen over the
  key lookup. Costing a range by its prefix as well keeps both: raft's work is flat, Kafka's fetch keeps the chunk
  index. The planner's cached join orders are keyed by
  `Store::size_class`, which for a tiered table includes an epoch that moves when an average crosses a power of two:
  an order planned on stale estimates is planned again.
- **Kept ranges.** The hot tier keeps range probes of large prefixes too (`KeptRange`: bounds and sorted rows, at most
  8 per prefix), kept current like the rest (a caught-up row joins or leaves every kept range it falls in).

Kafka, 300000 records of 1 KiB, two rounds each (in-memory against tiered): produce 6253 and 5716 against 5460 and
5684 records/s; end-to-end p50 4 against 5 ms, p99 22/36 against 39/63 ms (S24's tiered: p50 9 ms).

Tests: `node_tiered` adds a hot tier whose probes keep at most 10 rows (the whole table a large prefix, its small
ranges kept); a mutation that stops kept ranges following caught-up rows fails it.
