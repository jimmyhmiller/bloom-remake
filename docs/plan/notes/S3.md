# S3 — real processes

Slice 3 of docs/design/SLICES.md: a Blossom program runs as a real server. The sans-IO node over the oracle, durable
tables over the write-ahead log, recovery, a TCP transport, `blossom run`, and a client API. The demo is e01, the
key-value store, which never loses an acknowledged write when it is killed with `kill -9`.

## Evidence

- `tests/integration/tests/node_kvs.rs`: e01 on the node over `SimFs`.
  - A crash at **every durable syscall** of a workload that includes checkpoints keeps every acknowledged put.
    The test is mutation-checked: releasing before the WAL sync fails it.
  - Recovery replays the WAL on top of a checkpoint.
  - A torn tail loses only the unacknowledged tick.
  - Tick numbers are never reused, and the clock never goes back across incarnations.
  - Invariant R holds under random interleavings of ticks and sync reports (a property test).
- `crates/blossom-cli/tests/it/kill9.rs`: `blossom run` serves e01 under 8 clients with puts, gets and deletes.
  - The test SIGKILLs and restarts the process 5 or more times in 14 seconds.
  - The whole history, including the operations in flight at each kill, is linearizable.
  - It is mutation-checked: releasing a tick's replies before the committer writes its record is caught.
- `blossom-sim::linearize`: unit cases, a property test (real executions are linearizable, and a corrupted read is
  not), and a differential test against brute force on 3,000 random histories.

## Deviations

- **Evaluator.** The architecture's `Evaluator` is word-level: the engine ingests encoded batches. Until the engine
  exists it is value-level, `TickInput → TickOutput`, which is the oracle's interface. The node, the simulator and
  the fast interpreter to come all implement it.
- **Threads, not tokio.** The runtime uses blocking std threads: one reader per connection, one writer per peer and
  per session, plus the engine, committer and checkpoint threads. The channels between them are std `mpsc`, not
  `rtrb` rings. This is enough for a few nodes and a benchmark's clients. Revisit it if the etcd comparison shows the
  I/O layer, not evaluation, is the bottleneck.
- **Admission runs on the engine thread**, not in the reader. `principal in REL` reads the node's committed state.
- **Client `HELLO` carries the claimed principal** (`Peer::Client { principal }`). Only the insecure development
  transport accepts it unauthenticated. mTLS (DIST-060) fails as `Unimplemented`.
- **Handshake.** The acceptor answers `HELLO` with its own `HELLO` (its identity and its channel list) and then
  `HELLO_OK` (the opener's schema ids it accepts). A channel whose schema hash differs is left out, and its batches
  are dropped and counted.
- **Encoding is in the driver.** `run_tick` returns the durable delta and the outbox as values, and the driver encodes
  them. The architecture encodes them inside the node.
- **Tick faults.** A faulted node exits with code 6 and the supervisor restarts it. Probation and poison isolation
  (§5.12) are a later slice. A poisonous client message cannot crash-loop the node, because ingress is not durable.
- **Graceful shutdown.** SIGTERM is not handled, so it is a crash, which is always legal. The GOAWAY drain and the
  `clean_shutdown` flag are a later slice.
- **Deployment statics** apply to every node. Per-node static rows are not needed yet.
- **`deploy.toml` additions.** `node.client_addr` is the node's client listener. `deployment.source` is the program
  root, compiled at startup. `[statics]` holds the rows of `static` relations.
- **Checkpoint policy.** A checkpoint starts when the synced WAL has grown past `storage.checkpoint_wal_bytes` since
  the last one. The engine encodes the snapshot, which costs a pause proportional to the durable state.

## Found along the way

- **e01 was not linearizable.** Concurrent deletes of one key in one tick all reported `existed = true`. The checker
  found it on the first real run. The fix is a view, `del_winner`, that picks one delete per key per tick with
  `choose_most!((c, id) per key)`. The others are ordered after it and report `false`.
- **Relative data directories.** `durable_dir` fsynced the empty path `""` as the parent of a relative path's first
  component. It now syncs `.`, and deployment paths are made absolute.

## Follow-ups

- The sim-on-node path: run `SyncWorld` over `Node` and `SimFs` rather than calling the oracle directly, so the
  simulator exercises recovery and Invariant R.
- `session_open` / `session_closed` / `recovered()` event relations. The frontend does not produce them yet, and the
  node refuses them if it meets them.
- Per-principal quotas, the poison deny-list and probation.
- The IR validator does not check `AclExplicit` yet: that its roles exist and that `principal_in` is unary with a
  `Principal` column. The frontend checks both.
- `#[accept(principal in R)]` with no role or `external` is refused (BLS0210). The IR has no way to say "every
  node", and §18.3 reads as if sources are required.
- The corpus areas `net` and `lprov` are outside every gate so far, and have been stale since before S3 (the S2
  merge shows the same):
  - BENCH-100, BENCH-101, BENCH-102c and nine lprov cases pass, but their manifests still say `unimplemented`.
  - BENCH-309a/c and BENCH-311a–c fail to compile (`LMap::lt!`, a stratification error, company control).
  - A slice that takes on those areas should ratchet the first group and triage the second.

## Adversarial review

An independent review found 16 problems, 11 of them confirmed with failing tests. None lost an acknowledged write:
the replay and checkpoint coverage logic held up. All 16 are fixed (commit 23c609d), and the reproductions are now
regression tests (`blossom-store/tests/torn_tails.rs`, `node_kvs.rs`, `runtime_server.rs`).

- **Recovery refused after ordinary crashes.**
  - A torn first batch of a segment, or a batch after a batch-number gap, was called corruption. A torn record is
    now corruption only when a later whole record provably belongs to a synced batch.
  - A newest segment whose header never became durable blocked recovery for good. With no receipt it is an aborted
    creation, and recovery removes it.
- **Time went back across a restart.** Records covered by a checkpoint dropped their `now`, and ticks with no WAL
  record never persisted theirs. Release is now gated on a durable time bound, `META.last_now`, reserved one second
  ahead like the tick reservation. Restarts boot after it. The cluster simulator gives each incarnation a clock
  offset, as the real clock's anchoring does.
- **One large tick faulted the node.** Its WAL record passed the 64 MiB record limit. Batches are now bounded by
  bytes as well as by count.
- **Runtime:**
  - A file descriptor leaked per connection.
  - Replies were never split into frames under the limit.
  - Checkpoints were never removed, and every restart re-checkpointed.
  - Errors after startup exited as "refused".
  - There was no backpressure, and the engine could spin at 100% CPU.
  - `Stop` could be lost.
  - Handshakes and writes had no timeouts.
  - The client lost bytes when a read timed out mid-frame.
- **Tests that could not fail:**
  - The crash-point test now uses lost, kept, torn and mixed fates, and crashes again during recovery.
  - Malformed replies are protocol errors that fail a run; before, they counted as unanswered operations the checker
    would accept.
  - The kill -9 test cannot detect a missing fsync (the page cache survives SIGKILL). That is the crash-point test's
    job, and it is mutation-checked for exactly that.

## Performance note (for the etcd comparison)

A group commit costs four F_FULLFSYNCs:
1. the data;
2. the sync marker;
3. and 4. the receipt's atomic write (the file and the directory).

The last three detect corruption in acknowledged tail records; they add nothing to durability. etcd uses one
F_FULLFSYNC per batch (`fileutil.Fdatasync` on darwin). The comparison will report both configurations honestly, or
the store will get a cheaper way to certify the tail.
