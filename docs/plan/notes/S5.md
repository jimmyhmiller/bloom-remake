# S5 — the fast engine (plan)

Step 3 of the flagship goal: "An incremental, indexed evaluator replaces the naive reference oracle for execution, and
must agree with it on the whole corpus. It comes before the benchmark, because running the naive oracle on the
network would make the etcd comparison meaningless."

## Why the oracle is slow

Each tick, the oracle:
1. builds a fresh database from the carried state and the statics, re-indexing everything;
2. evaluates every stratum by naive iteration;
3. re-derives every persistent table through its frame rule (`t(x̄)@next :- t(x̄), notin t$del(x̄)`);
4. returns the full instance and the full next state.

So a tick costs O(state), whatever changed. For the Raft KV the log grows without bound, and so does every tick.

## Design

The engine is a value-level evaluator (`blossom-engine`) behind the node's `Evaluator` seam. Like the oracle, it
works on `Value` rows. The architecture's word-level kernel and code generation remain later work. Its principles:

- **State lives across ticks.** Every relation is stored once, with its indexes, and updated in place from changes,
  never rebuilt.
- **Everything is a change.** Across ticks, each relation is a Z-set (a set with signed multiplicities, as in
  DBSP), and a tick's work is the change to every relation. The change of a tick-local relation (events, channel
  receives, ingress, the tick's derived scratch) is its new content minus its old one. The change of a carried
  relation is what its inductive rules derive for the next tick, minus what they derived last time.
- **Rules are maintained, not re-derived.** A deductive rule's output changes by the delta query of its body:
  `Δ(A ⋈ B) = ΔA ⋈ B_old + A_new ⋈ ΔB`, with negation as a counted antijoin, and guards and bindings as maps and
  filters. Derivation counts keep set semantics exact under deletions (DBSP's distinct). Each regime is chosen per
  rule and per stratum:
  - **Counted** (delta queries): a non-recursive stratum with plain insert heads.
  - **Aggregates**: per-group state (value counts); a change touches only its groups. Covers count, sum, min, max,
    `argmin!`/`argmax!`, and choice (min over the seeded priority).
  - **Lattice cells**: per-cell contribution counts, with the cell's value recomputed as the join of its live
    contributions when they change.
  - **Recompute**: a recursive stratum, or a rule that reads a time-varying scalar (`$now`, `$tick`, `rand`,
    `choose_rand!`). It is re-evaluated in full, semi-naively, when its inputs changed or a time-varying scalar is
    read, and its output is diffed to produce the change.
- **Indexes.** Hash indexes serve equality probes and ordered (BTree) indexes serve ranges. A join keyed by an
  expression, such as `log(j, …) where j + 1 == i`, probes with the key computed from bound variables.
- **Outputs.** The outbox (async heads) and egress are the full contents of their head relations for the tick, which
  are small tick-local sets. The next-state change is the change of the inductive heads.

## The seam changes

- The tick's inputs and outputs (`Row`, `Instance`, `Delivery`, `Send`, `Ingress`, `Egress`, `TickInput`,
  `TickOutput`) move from `blossom-oracle` to `blossom-ir`, so the engine can share them without depending on the
  oracle (ARCH-16: the engine and the oracle share no evaluation code).
- The `Evaluator` becomes stateful: `reset(carried)` at boot, and `tick(&mut self, input)`. `TickOutput` gains the
  next state's change (`next_changes`). The node keeps its durable image with those changes instead of diffing whole
  instances, and asks for the full instance only when capturing (simulation, LDFI, tests).
- The oracle stays stateless: its `Evaluator` implementation diffs `carried` against `next` (O(state), as it always
  was).

## The gate

- **Differential:** the engine's instance, next state, outbox, egress and program errors equal the oracle's at
  every tick. This holds on every corpus case the oracle runs (the corpus runner's `interp` backend), and on every
  example program under random inputs.
- **Execution:** the node runs on the engine in `blossom run` and in the cluster simulator. The S3 and S4 tests
  (kill -9, the Raft KV in simulation and as three processes) pass on it.
- **Speed:** a tick costs O(change × fan-out × log n), not O(state), and the Raft KV's tick time stays flat as its
  log grows.
