# S13: the asynchronous fault model

**Goal (user, 2026-10-05):** LDFI and enumeration see only the synchronous world (a message sent in round `t`
arrives in round `t + 1` or is lost), so timing bugs are invisible to them: the full tier, not LDFI, found the
broker's quiescence bug (two `quiesce` messages of one sender delivered in one round). The asynchronous model adds
delays, and with them reordering.

The design docs specify only the synchronous LDFI (ARCHITECTURE §8.3, "Molly round k is tick k"); the closest
precedent is the ASP encoding's "a message arrives in (s, s+Δ] or is lost" (§9.2). This slice defines the rest.

## Decisions (user, 2026-10-05)

- **A delay delays a round batch:** `D(from, to, send, d)`: everything `from` sends `to` in round `send` arrives in
  round `send + d`, `d` in `2..=delay` (the spec's `delay: Δ`), like an omission loses one. Batches of different
  rounds arriving out of order are the reordering.
- **Streams are delayed too:** a stream's flight delayed holds back what follows it on that connection (TCP keeps
  order).
- **A budget:** `delays: k` in `faults`, default 1.

## Plan

1. The round world delivers each batch at its arrival round (channels; stream flights in connection order).

3. Enumeration over delays (the stepped certifier refuses the asynchronous model).
4. LDFI: delay atoms; lineage with arrival rounds; a delay falsifies an arrival on time, and makes a tuple appear
