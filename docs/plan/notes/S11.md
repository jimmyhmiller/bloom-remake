# S11: LDFI over the whole Kafka broker, under crash-restart (working notes)

The user's goal (2026-10-04): "get all the Molly stuff working for this so we can really have confidence that this is
correct." Choices (question tool): the **whole broker directly** (not the Raft layer first), and **crash-restart from
the start** (not Molly's crash-stop). Branch `slice-11`, worktree `.worktrees/s11`, from main after S10 (aae7510).

## Where LDFI stands

- Molly parity on `.ded` programs (S1), Blossom programs with `spec … for Module` (e04, e12: BENCH-137a–h), on the
  synchronous-round world (`blossom-sim::sync`, `spec`), the protocol run on the **oracle** (lineage needs every
  firing; the engine records none).
- Faults: omissions before EFF, crashes under `Frozen` (CR-20) or `MollyContinue`. Crash-recovery is TEST-037 (P1,
  "experimental"): R10 §A3.4(8), "a crash deletes non-durable relations and a restart resumes persistence from durable
  ones". Molly lists crash-recovery as future work (R06 §2.2): it is a loss window *and* a loss of ephemeral state.
- Refused today, and used by the broker: guarded timers in lineage (LANG-172; the world already simulates them),
  host streams (the broker's every request; the world has none).
- Feasibility (measured): the oracle runs the broker at ~1.6 ms a tick (the engine 0.11 ms): a 40-round run of four
  nodes is ~160 ticks, under a second before capture. Lineage size and the SAT encoding at this scale are the open
  questions.

## Plan

1. **Crash-restart in the synchronous world.** A fault `restart(n, t, t')`: `n` does not run rounds `t..t'`; messages
   to it are lost and it sends none; at `t'` it boots with the durable relations as they were after round `t − 1`
   (a round is atomic: a crash in a round loses that round, as a crash before its WAL sync does), every volatile
   relation empty, `boot()` and `recovered()`, its timers re-armed.
2. **Streams in the synchronous world.** Node-to-node connections on a node's streams (dial, accept, `opened`,
   `data`, writes delivered the next round, `closed`), in `seq` order; a crash closes its connections at both ends.
3. **A spec over the broker.** The broker and the Blossom Kafka client as a spec target, the client driven by spec
   facts (produce, fetch), with the Kafka properties as `pre`/`post`: an `acks=all` batch is never lost and holds its
   acknowledged offset on every replica that is up; an acknowledged batch is fetched back; an idempotent batch is
   stored once.
4. **LDFI lineage and hazards** for the new world: stream data as message-like premises; guarded timer firings
   derived from their guards; restart variables (the frames of volatile relations break at a restart, firings and
   sends stop while down, the crash budget counts restarts). Validated on small programs first: a toy protocol that
   acknowledges before its write is durable must give a counterexample, the correct one must hold.
5. **The broker under LDFI**: the failure-free run, its lineage, the search; seeded bugs (a produce answered before
   its commit, a leader counting its own unsynced copy, an in-sync replica not waited for) must be found, the
   correct broker must hold at its bounds.
6. **Performance** as the runs require (capture cost, snapshot resume, parallel runs).
