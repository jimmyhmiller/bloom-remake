## Decisions

- **D1. One Raft layer, many groups.** Replication is Raft (as KAFKA.md decides), written once in Blossom
  (`examples/kafka/raft.bls`) and keyed by a group, `Group = (topic id, partition)`. The controller is the group of
  the reserved metadata topic id (0, 1), partition 0, whose members are every broker; each topic partition is a group
  whose members are its replica set. Elections, replication, commit and truncation are the same code for both; only
  what an entry means differs.
- **D2. Brokers are deployment configuration.** `static broker(n: Node<Broker>, id: i32, host: String, port: i32)`
  rows come from the deployment spec, so every broker knows every broker's id and address from the start. There is
  no dynamic registration (Kafka's brokers register to report liveness; the controller's own Raft membership is
  enough here). Recorded as a deviation.
- **D3. Entries.** `rlog(g, idx, term, prev, kind, pos, data: Blob)`: Raft index and term, the term of the entry
  before (the consistency check), the entry's kind (DATA: a record batch; NOOP: a new leader's first entry, so it
  commits its predecessors; CMD: a metadata command; CONFIG: a membership change, for reassignment), its position (a
  DATA entry's Kafka base offset, fixed by the leader when it appends), and its bytes as a blob. A Kafka offset is
  not a Raft index (KAFKA.md).
- **D4. Bytes on the wire.** A `Blob` cannot travel in a channel yet (FOREIGN-PROTOCOLS §5a). AppendEntries carries
  an entry's bytes (`blob.read`), and the follower stores `Blob::of` of them: the same blob. Shipping blobs natively
  is a later performance step.
- **D5. Offsets.** A partition's high watermark is the end offset of its last committed DATA entry; its log end is the
  end of its last DATA entry. The leader epoch is the group's term.
- **D6. Producer state is derived from the log.** Per partition and producer, the last five DATA entries of the
  producer (a view over the log), so a truncation or a failover rebuilds it with no extra state.
- **D7. Leadership is announced.** Each partition leader tells every broker which groups it leads, with their
  epochs, at each heartbeat; every broker keeps the highest epoch it has heard per group for Metadata. A stale view
  only costs a client a NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER and a metadata refresh, as in Kafka.
- **D8. Admin writes go through the controller.** CreateTopics, DeleteTopics and InitProducerId's id blocks are
  commands appended to the metadata group by its leader. The broker a client asked forwards its command to the
  controller leader; every broker applies committed commands in order and the outcomes (a topic created, a name
  taken) are decided there, deterministically. The asking broker answers when its own apply reaches its command.
- **D9. acks.** `acks=all`: answered when the partition's high watermark passes the batch. `acks=1`: answered once
  the leader's append is durable (the tick is released after its sync). `acks=0`: no answer.
- **D10. Reads from the leader.** Produce, Fetch and ListOffsets go to the partition leader; any other broker answers
  NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER. Fetch reads up to the high watermark. A request's `current_leader_epoch` older than the
  leader's is FENCED_LEADER_EPOCH, a newer one UNKNOWN_LEADER_EPOCH (KIP-320).
- **D11. Placement.** The controller assigns each new partition's replicas round-robin from a seeded start, as
  Kafka's rack-unaware assignment does; the first replica is the preferred leader.
- **D12. Retention per replica.** Each replica deletes old segments on its own clock. A follower whose next entry is
  below the leader's log start is told so, drops its log and resumes from the leader's start (the snapshot of a
  partition log is its start offset).

## Findings and deviations

### Item 1: the replicated log (done)

- **`examples/kafka/raft.bls`**: e11's rules, keyed by group and run over a member relation. Per group: durable term,
  vote and log; volatile leader state, commit index and election deadline. The includer gives `members(g, n)` and
  `proposals(g, xs)` (one ordered vector per group and tick: `index!` with `per` is not implemented, and the partition
  layer orders its appends anyway). A new leader appends a NOOP, so inherited entries commit without a client's entry.
  Quorums count a group's members (`count!` against the group's size), so no language change was needed for
  "majority over a relation".
- **The harness and its checks** (`tests/integration/fixtures/raft/groups.bls`, `raft_groups.rs`): five groups of
  sizes 4, 3, 3, 2 and 1 over four brokers, each leader appending on a timer, under loss, partitions, crashes
  (some between append and sync) and downtime. A per-group observer checks election safety, log matching (each
  entry's `prev`), state machine safety over committed entries, leader completeness, and that a leader's record of
  a follower is true while the follower is in its term. Every group commits under every seed.
- **Mutation checks.** Votes granted without the up-to-date check: caught (state machine safety). A leader
  committing its whole log alone: caught. Two mutations turned out not to be bugs here: committing with half the
  group (a commit quorum of 2 of 4 still meets every 3-vote quorum), and committing an index on one follower's
  acknowledgement (a leader only commits an index some follower acknowledged, which is then on 2 brokers). **Not
  caught:** keeping an earlier leadership's follower records (the S4 review's bug). The S4 directed scenario does not
  set up here, since a new leader's NOOP reaches the follower and makes it, not the old leader, the most up-to-date;
  the record-accuracy check did not catch it under random faults either. The reset stays (Raft resets `matchIndex`
  on election); the test that kills this mutant is still owed.
- **Platform:** `Observer::due` lets an observer check every few milliseconds of simulated time instead of every
  step (building every node's state per step dominated the run); `Cluster::input` gives a node an input, for directed
  tests.

### Items 2 and 3: the controller and partition replication (done)

- **`examples/kafka/cluster.bls`** is the controller: the metadata group over every broker (`static broker` rows,
  D2), its commands, and every broker's applied metadata (`mtopic`, `massign`, `mdeleted`, `outcome`, `next_pid`),
  volatile and replayed from the log after a restart. A command is applied once per (origin, tag); a broker sends
  its command again every `CMD_RESEND` until its own apply reaches it. Tags and topic ids are `hash64` of the request
  (connection, index, entry), so a command sent again is the same command: `rand` is per tick (BLS0601), and a fresh
  id per tick made the awaited command change every tick (a livelock the simulator's new guard reported).
- **A request is one command.** A CreateTopics, DeleteTopics or auto-creating Metadata request is a single command
  whose items are its entries, applied in one tick. One command per entry let another request's command land
  between two entries of one request, which no order of whole requests explains (Kafka's controller handles a request
  in one event); the one-tick linearizability test found it. Items of one request name distinct topics (duplicates
  and collisions within a request are refused before any command), so each is decided against the metadata before
  the command.
- **Decided where.** Checks that come before a taken name (a name twice, illegal, colliding within the request) are
  answered by the broker a client asked. The others ride in the command as the item's error: at apply, a taken name
  wins, else that error, else the topic is created. A validate-only creation reports the zero id (as Kafka). A
  deletion resolves its names once, when it is first at the head (`delete_pinned`), so its own command does not
  change what it named. An auto-creating Metadata request pins the names it may create; the controller creates
  those still free, and the request is answered from the metadata just after its command, so a name freed meanwhile
  is created too.
- **`caught_up()` is a latch.** A broker serves stateful requests once it has applied the controller log up to a
  commit index learned in this incarnation, and from then on answers from what it has applied. Gating on "applied ≥
  commit" every tick held a request back whenever the controller committed ahead, so it was answered from a later
  state than its own command's.
- **`examples/kafka/raft.bls` additions:** every member pre-votes term 1 for the group's preferred member
  (`preferred(g, n)`, D11), which leads at once, so a new partition has a leader without an election; a size-1 group
  arms its election at once. The leader epoch clients see is the term less one (the first leader's is 0).
- **`produce_node.bls`:** Produce appends DATA entries to the partition group at its leader (the base offset fixed
  at append, producer state = the committed producers plus the uncommitted tail, D6). Committed entries are
  materialized per replica into S7's tables (batches, segments, time index, producers, high watermark). acks=1 is
  answered at the append tick, acks=all when the high watermark passes the batch, acks=0 never; an entry whose term
  was lost answers NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER. Fetch and ListOffsets answer at the leader only, with leader-epoch checks
  (D10). InitProducerId allocates through a controller command.
- **Fixes to S7 found on the way:** UNKNOWN_LEADER_EPOCH is 75 (S7 had 76, which is UNSUPPORTED_COMPRESSION_TYPE).
- **Platform:** `hash64(x)` builtin (the value's fingerprint, both evaluators); the simulator's livelock guard
  (`LIVELOCK_TICKS`) reports the relations that keep changing.
- **Tests:** the single-broker S7 suites run against the cluster program with one broker (`kafka_brokers` in the
  test library makes `bN` broker N). Clients treat NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER and REQUEST_TIMED_OUT as retriable, as
  stock clients do; an idempotent client retries the same batch.

### Item 4: Metadata across the cluster (done; multi-broker checks come with item 6)

- **In-sync replicas.** Each partition leader keeps, per follower, when it was last caught up (acknowledged the
  commit index; a new leadership starts with every replica caught up), and counts in sync the followers caught up
  within `REPLICA_LAG_MAX` (Kafka's `replica.lag.time.max.ms`, 30 s). The leader announces its groups with their ISR
  at each heartbeat; every broker keeps the latest announcement per group, ordered by (epoch, the leader's send
  time), since one leader sends every announcement of an epoch. Metadata reports leader, epoch, replicas and ISR.
  `acks=all` is refused with NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS when the ISR is below `min.insync.replicas` (S7 compared with the
  replica count).
- **DescribeCluster (60), v0–2.** The brokers (none fenced, D2), the cluster id and the controller as Metadata
  reports it; asked for controllers, a broker answers UNSUPPORTED_ENDPOINT_TYPE, as Kafka's brokers do. Authorized
  operations, when asked, are every operation of the resource (Kafka without an authorizer); Metadata's topic
  operations likewise. `kafka-cluster.sh cluster-id` reads the cluster id through it (gate).
- **Deviations.** `offline_replicas` is always empty and every broker is listed: brokers are configuration, with no
  registration or liveness (D2).
- **Language:** a resolution cost must be one column (LANG-117), so the announcement's order is one tuple-typed
  column `stamp: (u64, Instant)`; `upsert` into a resolved table is not implemented either, so those writes are
  `next`.

### Item 6: simulated cluster checks (in progress)

- **`kafka_cluster.rs`**: 3 and 5 brokers (each partition on 3) in the cluster simulator, with Rust clients that
  behave as Kafka's: create the topic through any broker, learn leaders from Metadata, send each Produce to its
  partition's leader, and on NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER, a timeout or a lost connection refresh their metadata (an
  idempotent producer resends the same batch). The Raft observer (`raft_safety`, now shared with `raft_groups.rs`)
  runs all along. After the faults stop and the cluster settles: one log per partition on every replica; every
  acks=all acknowledgement at its offset; acks=1 there or nowhere; only sent batches, each once; unique producer ids.
- **Bug found: a duplicate answered before its original committed.** An idempotent producer's retried batch found
  as a duplicate of an *uncommitted* entry was answered at once, so `acks=all` acknowledged a batch a new leader then
  truncated (five brokers, seed 3). The duplicate now waits for the high watermark to pass the original, under the
  leadership that found it (Kafka's DelayedProduce waits the same way), and fails NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER if that
  leadership ends.
- **Mutation checks:** the duplicate fix reverted (caught: OUT_OF_ORDER_SEQUENCE, then acknowledged data missing);
  `acks=all` answered at the append (caught: an acknowledged batch missing); a leader committing alone in a group
  of 3 (caught: state machine safety).
- **A consumer** (`Reader`) fetches every partition from offset 0 at its leader with the leader epoch Metadata gave,
  across leader changes: what it reads comes in order with no gap and is in the final log at that offset (it read
  only committed data), and it reads every batch by the end.
- **Bug found: Fetch's range ended at the high watermark.** Kafka allows a fetch offset up to the leader's log end
  and only shows records up to the high watermark; a new leader's high watermark can lag what its predecessor
  exposed, and an `acks=1` producer is answered before its followers copy the batch. Both fetched past the high
  watermark and got OFFSET_OUT_OF_RANGE, which resets a real consumer's position. The range now ends at the log end
  (`tail_end`). Checked by a probe: without faults, each `acks=1` acknowledgement is followed by a fetch right after
  the batch, which must not fail (mutation-checked).
- **Clients follow leader epochs (KIP-320).** A broker cut off from the rest still believes it leads and says so in
  Metadata; the test clients, as Kafka's, ignore metadata with an older leader epoch than one seen, and after a
  refusal want a newer one. (Found when the reader followed such a broker back to epoch 0.)
- **Admin requests through any of three brokers** (`kafka_topics.rs`, crashes, splits, one-way cuts): creations
  (some validate-only) and deletions are linearizable, and every broker ends with the same topics and each replica
  with its partitions. Metadata and DescribeConfigs read a broker's own copy, which may lag (as in Kafka), so those
  runs leave them out. Found and fixed on the way:
  - **Decisions on stale local state.** A validate-only creation checked taken names against the asking broker's
    metadata, and a deletion resolved names to ids there; in KRaft both go to the controller. Both are now commands
    (`OP_VALIDATE`; deletion items carry the name or the id, resolved when applied), so the controller's order
    decides them. Deletions no longer pin anything: their command is a function of the request.
  - **Topic ids collided across brokers.** Ids were `hash64` of (connection, request, entry), and a connection id is
    unique on its broker only; two brokers minted the same id, and a deleted topic's partition kept coming back (the
    livelock guard caught it). The asking broker's id is now part of the hash.
  - **Raft caught followers up one entry at a time.** Each entry was its own message, and a follower took an entry
    only after its predecessor; reordered messages (the simulator's per-message latency, any datagram network) were
    dropped, so a follower that fell behind regained a few entries per heartbeat and its metadata lagged for seconds.
    AppendEntries now carries a batch (up to `RAFT_BATCH` entries after an index and its term) that a follower takes
    whole, as Raft specifies. `raft_groups` runs a third faster.
  - **Applied metadata is durable** (with its index), as a Kafka broker keeps its metadata log and snapshot: a broker
    that restarted while cut off answered from metadata it was still replaying (UNKNOWN_TOPIC_OR_PARTITION for a
    topic it had known). And the "caught up" latch now waits for the commit index the controller's leader announced
    (`heard_commit`), not the lower one a follower can confirm, as Kafka fences a broker until it reaches the
    controller's high watermark.
- **Platform: a `match` in a rule's `let` was rejected as invalid IR.** The IR validator counted a match arm's
  pattern variables as free in the expression, so the `let` never bound (an internal error, not a diagnostic). It now
  leaves out variables an expression scopes itself (match arms, `let` in fn bodies, closure parameters); both
  evaluators already handled it (`bls_functions` checks one now).
- **Clean-up found on the way:** a deleted topic's Raft state (term, vote, leadership, commit, follower records) now
  goes with it; its leader kept announcing it before.

### D12: compaction and catching up from a snapshot (done)

- **`raft.bls`** gains Raft's compaction (§7): the includer names `compactable(g, i, pos)` entries; a broker drops
  them and keeps the snapshot point `rsnap(g, idx, term, pos)` (durable, only moving forward). `last_log`,
  `term_at`, a batch's and a beat's predecessor check all know the point (entries at or before it are committed, the
  same everywhere). A leader that can no longer send a follower its entries sends the point at each heartbeat
  (InstallSnapshot); the follower drops its log, takes the point and its commit, acknowledges it, and the includer
  resets the group's state (`install`).
- **Kafka:** an entry is compactable once applied and its batch deleted by retention, so the replication log holds
  only what the partition still holds. A follower installing a point empties its partition (batches, chunks, time
  index, segments, producer states) and restarts at the point's position, the leader's log start: what a Kafka
  follower does when it fetched below its leader's log start (`truncateFullyAndStartAt`). Producer states go too, as
  in Kafka; a producer the partition no longer knows may continue at any sequence.
- **Checks:** `kafka_retention` asserts the replication log is compacted with the data (no entry below the log
  start; the point is the start). `kafka_cluster`'s directed test stops a broker while its leaders' retention
  compacts past where it stopped (asserted), restarts it, and checks it took a point and holds what the others hold
  from its start to the same end. Mutation: no InstallSnapshot sent (caught: the logs differ). The Raft observer
  knows snapshot points (a compacted committed entry is not missing).
- **Client fix found on the way:** the test client kept dialling a dead leader; a lost or refused connection now
  sends it back to the metadata, as Kafka's clients do.

### Item 5: reassignment (done)

- **Raft membership changes** (`raft.bls`, §4.2–4.3, one server at a time): a CONFIG entry holds a configuration
  (member ids); a broker's members of a group are the latest configuration in its log, else its snapshot point's,
  else the includer's `initial_members`. The leader tracks a configuration's members (a member added gets its
  entries, or the snapshot point, from the start), counts only them toward commits, and steps down once a
  configuration leaving it out is committed. A broker that keeps a group's log without being in its configuration
  yet (`hosts`: a replica being added) takes entries; a vote request from outside a broker's configuration never
  raises its term. The snapshot point keeps the configuration, and InstallSnapshot carries it.
- **Kafka** (`reassign.bls`, `cluster.bls`, `topics_node.bls`): AlterPartitionReassignments (45, v0–1) is one
  controller command (the broker checks the replicas: distinct, existing, at least one); the controller records the
  target (`mreassign`) or answers UNKNOWN_TOPIC_OR_PARTITION, NO_REASSIGNMENT_IN_PROGRESS or
  INVALID_REPLICATION_FACTOR (v1's flag). The partition's leader changes its configuration one replica at a time:
  the target's first, then, once every target replica is in sync (the ISR), the others, itself last. When the
  configuration is the target it reports to the controller (`OP_REASSIGNED`), which makes the target the replicas.
  A broker a partition left drops its log, snapshot point and data (its term and votes stay). Metadata and
  ListPartitionReassignments (46) show a partition being reassigned with the target's replicas and then those it
  removes (adding and removing for List); a leader not among the replicas a broker knows is shown as none.
- **Deviation:** cancelling (null replicas) reassigns back to the replicas, through the same one-at-a-time changes,
  and shows as a reassignment until done (Kafka reverts at once: its adding replicas were never in the ISR quorum).
- **Checks:** `raft_groups` gains group `moving`, whose leader walks a schedule of configurations under the nemesis
  (each seed commits several); `kafka_cluster` moves every partition of a five-broker cluster twice under load,
  without and with faults: all checks of item 6 hold on the final replicas, every wave finishes, the partitions are
  where the last wave put them, and the brokers they left hold nothing of them. Codec round trips for 45 and 46.
  Mutation: no clean-up of a partition left (caught). **Not caught** (owed a directed scenario, like S4's): a
  configuration changing several members at once (at the Raft and the Kafka layer); the random faults never hit the
  window where two configurations differing by two have disjoint majorities.
