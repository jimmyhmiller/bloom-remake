# S26 — what memory holds, the restart, and the tail

The user (2026-10-07): "Let's fix those latency issues. Fix the reading every table whole. Fix all of this please",
after S25 reported the open items of the tiered engine: its p99 (39–63 ms against 22–36 ms in memory), its first
tick after a restart reading every tiered table whole, and memory barely smaller than the in-memory engine's.

## Measuring first

New stats (`blossom run --stats`): `tick_nanos`, `tick_max_nanos`, `ticks_over_4ms`, `ticks_over_16ms`,
`db_apply_nanos` / `db_apply_max_nanos` (the engine thread), `db_flush_nanos` / `db_flush_max_nanos` (the database
thread); `resident.<relation>.<store>` for the twelve stores holding the most rows (`Engine::resident_by_store`,
refreshed about once a second); `slow.<rule>.ticks|rows|steps`, the rules' work in ticks over 4 ms
(`Engine::last_tick_work`). `scripts/bench-kafka.sh` takes `TIERED=false`.

Kafka on computer.jimmyhmiller.com (three brokers, 300000 records of 1 KiB produced, then 3000 end-to-end), one
build, `storage.tiered` off and on:

- **The tail is not the tiering.** In memory: end-to-end p99 38 ms; tiered: 31 ms. Both have hundreds of ticks over
  4 ms and a longest near 28 ms; the earlier gap was run-to-run noise between two builds. The slow ticks' work is the
  Raft replication of the Kafka program (`incoming`, a follower taking a batch of up to 64 entries and hashing each
  into a blob; `out_rows`, `out_batch$u`, `out_batch$agg`, the leader building batches), in both.
- **Memory holds the views, not the tables.** In memory ~339000 rows, tiered ~347000: the tables are on disk, but
  the views over them are not: `batch_chunk$keep`, `term_at`, `holds_point`, `last_log$argmax#1$cand`, `data_rlog`,
  `data_entry`, `entry_index`, `data_last$u`, `readable_batch`, `batch$keep`, `time_index$keep`, ~23000 rows each
  (a row per log entry). They are also why the first tick after a restart reads every table whole: a view starts
  empty and is derived again from all its table's rows.

## Step 1: durable views (DATABASE.md §8)

- **Which.** `Engine::durable_views`: derived relations whose every running rule is deductive, kept by delta queries
  (no aggregate, recursion or reading of time), with no fact, reading tiered tables, static relations or other such
  views. Step 1 keeps those whose rows can hold no blob (a view's blob is not counted as the carried rows' are).
  Kafka: 40 relations, among them every guarded table's `$keep` (`batch$keep`, `batch_chunk$keep`, `time_index$keep`,
  …), `replica`, the `argmax`es' candidates.
- **Kept.** A durable view's store is tiered with support counts (`Tiered::counted`: the overlay holds counts, the
  hot tier and `ColdTables::support` too); `Store::take_changes` gives each tick's changed rows (support before and
  after) as `StepOutput::views`, which the node carries in `ReleasedTick::views` (not in the WAL) and the database
  applies with the tick's delta (`Database::apply_tick`: a row key when it comes or goes, a count key for a support
  of two or more). Keyspaces are named by the view's definition hash and a generation (`view_tags`); a definition key
  is written with the view's complete rows (`ColdTables::open_views`).
- **Restart.** `Resume` (the deployment's statics, and `CatchUp`) goes to `Executor::reset_on`. Recovery gives the
  WAL's deltas from the database's flushed tick on (`catch_up_of`: the net change of all but the last, and the last);
  the WAL now keeps the flushed tick's record (`FileWal::truncation`). With the views complete and a catch-up, the
  engine loads the statics and facts without showing them as a change, and its first step runs `Engine::catch_up`
  (the tiered tables rewound to the tick before the last, showing the net change; only the views' rules), then the
  boot tick as any tick, deriving what memory lost in full (`rebuilding`: full evaluation for every rule that is not a
  view's, recursive strata dirty). Else (a new definition, a database started here) the views start empty in a new
  generation and are built at the first tick, as before.
- **A bug the checked backend found.** A tiered table's frame compared its next state with one recomputed from the
  last tick's deletions, which a restart loses (tick-local): rows deleted by the last tick before a restart were
  deleted again. A tiered table's next state as the last tick left it is its carry (`Store::carried_in`), in every
  tick.
- **One hot budget.** `EngineConfig::hot_rows` is now the engine's total (65536 rows by default), not each store's:
  with forty views each keeping 16384 rows, the hot tiers outgrew what tiering saved. At each tick's end the largest
  hot tier gives up half until they fit (`Engine::balance_hot`).

Kafka, a broker killed (`kill -9`) after 300000 records and restarted: resident rows after the restart 263614 (S25)
against 188487; the boot tick still derives the views that are not durable yet (877 ms): `term_at`, `holds_point`,
`data_rlog`, `data_entry`, `entry_index`, `readable_batch`, which mix durable and volatile rules or depend on an
aggregate (steps 2–4).

Tests: `node_tiered` through restarts with the checked backend covers a view (`big`) and a guard (`watched$keep`); a
mutation that skips the catch-up's view rules fails it. The simulator suites under `BLOSSOM_EVALUATOR=checked`.

## Step 3: durable tables kept in memory as sources

The durable tables the engine keeps in memory (`resolve`, sealed, lattice: loaded from the database at a reset
onto it, `Engine::durable_in_memory`) are sources of durable views as tiered tables are. On a resume they start as
the views' version had them (the WAL's changes since undone, loaded without showing as a change); the catch-up shows
the net change of all but the last tick, and the first tick takes the last as their pending change (the baseline,
their rows as recovered, still gives the first tick's change). Fixture: `floor_at` (`resolve choose_most`) and the
view `above` over it and `kv`; a mutation that skips their catch-up fails the checked test.

## Step 2: views with blobs, aggregates, and mixed relations

- **Purity instead of "no time".** `purity.rs`: a view's rules may read no time (`$now`, `$tick`, `$incarnation`,
  `$host`), draw nothing random and make no blob (`Blob::of`); user functions are followed into their bodies. A view
  may now hold a blob a row it reads holds: the node counts a durable view's rows' blobs as the carried rows' are
  (`view_presence`), and the database keeps blob keys for view keyspaces too.
- **Aggregates.** A view whose sole rule is a single-atom aggregate (`count`, `min`, `max`, `sum`, `collect` without
  an order) is durable (`AggShape`): its groups are not held in memory. `max` and `min` move from the current head row
  where they can; the others, and a retraction of the extreme, read the group again from the source
  (`group_from_source`). A stratum's aggregates catch up before its rules, as at any tick (a bug the checked test
  found: an aggregate's count was off after a restart).
- **Mixed relations.** A relation with durable rules and others (a view over a tiered table and one over a volatile
  table): its durable rules' support is kept in the database, counted; the others' support is kept in memory
  (`Store::add_by(row, w, volatile)`) and derived again at the boot tick (`Engine::mixed_relations`). Not a source of
  another durable view: part of it is lost on a restart. Kafka: `holds_point`, `readable_batch`.
- **The database's count reads.** `view_support` read a row's count key with a one-key page, which could be a
  tombstone: a count of two or more read as one (found by `raft_groups` under the checked backend: a peer missing
  from a view). It pages until a live key.
- **The checked backend names what differs.** A mismatch reports the relations whose rows differ at the tick
  (`Engine::instance_now` against the oracle's instance), not only the first differing output.

## Step 4: what a tick wrote into the views' sources

The checked test then failed after a third restart: a guard's `$keep` row counted twice. A view's rows at tick `t`
are computed from its sources' rows at the tick, which are the tables at `t-1` with what the tick's rules wrote: an
`emit` into a durable table shows the tick it is written (`armed`, `watched` are kept in memory for that reason), a
lattice row merges past its carried one. The catch-up assumed the views at the flushed tick `F` were those of the
tables at `F-1`, and counted tick `F`'s emitted rows again.

Each tick now reports, for the durable tables its views read (`Engine::view_sources`), how their rows differ from
those carried in (`StepOutput::written`: rows not carried, from the tiered store's `uncarried` set or a kept table's
change less the carry it started with; carried rows not present, a lattice's). The node logs it in the tick's WAL
record (`Delta::written`, as bits over the record's inserts and deletes, with the rows not among them; a record
written before has none), and logs a record for a tick that wrote such rows and changed nothing. `catch_up_of` starts
from tick `F`'s rows: a row's first change says whether the tables at `F-1` held it, tick `F`'s written rows correct
that, and its last change says whether the tables at `V-1` hold it.

Tests: `a_restart_catches_the_views_up_from_what_the_flushed_tick_wrote` (a flush at a tick that emits into a guarded
table and merges a lattice row, restarted there and two ticks later, checked backend); a mutation that drops the
written rows fails it with a negative support. Dropping only the carried rows a lattice merged past changes nothing
visible (the merged row dominates them in the restored lattice) and is not caught; they are logged as the exact
definition. `a_record_logs_the_rows_a_tick_wrote_beyond_the_carried_ones` round-trips the record.

## The rate and the tail

Kafka on the server (300000 records of 1 KiB, then 2000 end-to-end; three rounds each, alternating builds):

| build | produce (records/s) | e2e p99 (ms) | e2e p99.9 (ms) | resident rows | RSS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S25 tiered | 5600, 5474, 5528 | 38, 21, 45 | 820, 790, 814 | ~325000 | ~445 MB |
| S26 durable views | 4190, 4927, 4576 | 12, 21, 26 | 298, 332, 361 | ~70000 | 280–390 MB |
| + four-chain hash | 4956, 5397, 5088 | 11, 17, 30 | 287, 289, 331 | | |
| + 256 rows a probe | 5472, 5557, 5427 | 20, 20, 12 | 286, 289, 371 | | |

- **Hashing.** A fifth of a broker's time was `DetHasher::write` over rows holding records' bytes (a row is hashed
  whenever a store takes it). From 64 bytes on it now runs four chains over the words in turn, each mixed as before,
  folded in in order: 1 KiB in 52 ns, from 180.
- **One probe's rows.** With one engine budget, a probe kept up to 1024 rows (budget/64): an unknown prefix was read
  up to that before it was known large, and a kept answer is read whole. 256 at most (S25's per-table budget gave
  that) brings the produce rate back; a budget four times larger (1024 rows a probe, 4096 kept) cut it to 1800.
- **Restart** (a broker killed after the produce): ready after 0.9 s, its longest tick 100 ms (was 877 ms after step 1,
  and every tiered table read whole before S26), 24000 resident rows after (60000 before step 4); what stays is the
  log's overlay of rows the database has not caught up with (`rlog`, `rlog$del`).
